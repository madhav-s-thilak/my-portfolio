## Overview

ArcticRadar is a physics-driven, semi-supervised machine learning pipeline
for detecting liquid water on the surface of Antarctic ice sheets using
airborne VHF radar sounder data. Built on NASA's CReSIS MCoRDS 2016
Antarctica DC8 campaign, it processes **336,252 radar traces** across the
Antarctic Peninsula — requiring **zero manually labelled training examples**.

The project bridges cryospheric remote sensing, electromagnetic physics,
and modern ML: every feature is grounded in wave propagation theory, and
every algorithm decision is physically interpretable. Developed as a
Design-Oriented Project at BITS Pilani under the supervision of
**Dr. Sanchari Thakur**, whose own research on the STRATUS orbital radar
sounder mission provided the direct methodological foundation.

## The Problem

Antarctica holds enough ice to raise global sea levels by 70 metres.
Surface meltwater is a critical but poorly mapped driver of ice-sheet
instability — it drains to the ice bed (accelerating flow), forms
supraglacial lakes (destabilising ice shelves), and modifies albedo feedback.

Optical satellites are blinded by clouds and polar night.
**VHF radar sounders penetrate both** — but automated detection of meltwater
from radar echoes, without hand-labelled data, had not been attempted at this
scale before this project.

## Core Physics

The entire pipeline is anchored to one physical fact: **liquid water
reflects radar energy differently from ice**.

Water has dielectric permittivity ε ≈ 80 vs ice ε ≈ 3.15. This enormous
contrast produces:

- **High reflectivity** — 15 dB stronger echo than comparable dry ice
- **Narrow pulse** — specular reflection concentrates energy at one depth bin
- **High along-track coherence** — a flat liquid surface gives identical echoes in consecutive radar firings
- **High specularity** — coherent power Pc dominates over incoherent power Pn

Every one of the 16 features in the pipeline is a different mathematical
realisation of one of these four physical properties.

## Dataset

**Instrument**: NASA CReSIS MCoRDS (Multi-channel Coherent Radar Depth
Sounder), 195 MHz centre frequency, 30 MHz bandwidth, CSARP_mvdr
beamformer product.

**Campaign**: 2016 Antarctica DC8, Antarctic Peninsula, November 2016.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Radargrams processed | 100 (.mat files, 3 beams) |
| Total radar traces | 336,252 |
| Depth bins per trace | ~900 |
| Geographic coverage | Lat −68° to −74°, Antarctic Peninsula |
| Consensus meltwater | 16,352 traces (4.2%) |

Each trace is a vertical radar echo — a column of ~900 power measurements
representing depth from the aircraft to the ice bed. All 16 features are
computed per trace using the surface echo region.

## 16-Feature Physics Framework

Features are split into four groups, each targeting one physical signature
of meltwater.

### Group 1 — Amplitude & Energy
**Peak Power (dB)** `max(w_dB)` — maximum signal strength; specular water
produces ~15 dB stronger return than ice.

**Echo Energy (dB)** `10·log₁₀(Σ w_linear)` — total integrated energy;
complements peak power by capturing breadth vs height of the pulse.

**Echo Width (FWHM)** — full-width at half-maximum in linear power; a
specular meltwater echo is 3–8 bins wide vs 15–30 bins for rough ice.
*This is the highest-importance feature in both RF and XGBoost.*

### Group 2 — Waveform Shape
**Abruptness** `|∇w|_max / (w_max − w_min)` — leading edge steepness
normalised by dynamic range. A sharp ice–water interface gives a nearly
vertical wavefront.

**Skewness** `E[(w−μ)³] / σ³` — waveform asymmetry. Meltwater: rapid
rise, slow trailing edge → positive skew.

**Kurtosis** `E[(w−μ)⁴] / σ⁴ − 3` — peakedness vs Gaussian. Specular
echoes are leptokurtic.

**Leading / Trailing Edge Slopes** — mean positive gradients before the
peak and mean negative gradients after it. High values indicate a sharp,
narrow pulse with minimal volume scattering.

### Group 3 — Statistical & Spatial
**Coefficient of Variation** `σ(w_lin) / μ(w_lin)` — relative power
variability within the depth window.

**Signal Entropy** `−Σ p·log₂(p)` — Shannon entropy of the normalised
power distribution. Low entropy = energy concentrated at one bin = specular.

**Texture Variance** — variance of the 20×16 power patch (±10 depth bins,
±8 traces). Low variance = spatially homogeneous flat liquid surface.

**Local Contrast** `max(w) − mean(patch)` — how much this trace stands
out from its spatial neighbourhood. Detects isolated melt ponds and edges.

### Group 4 — RSR Coherent / Incoherent Decomposition (Primary Physics)
The Radar Surface Return (RSR) framework decomposes total echo power using
a 750-trace sliding window into two physically distinct components:

```
Pc = [mean(x)]²          ← Coherent power (squared mean amplitude)
Pn = mean(x²) − Pc       ← Incoherent power (variance of amplitude)
```

This decomposition is exact. A perfectly smooth surface has zero incoherent
power. A perfectly rough surface has zero coherent power.

**RSR Pc (dB)** — proxy for specular surface reflectivity.

**RSR Pn (dB)** — proxy for surface roughness and volume scattering.

**RSR Specularity** `Pc(dB) − Pn(dB)` — *the primary meltwater
discriminant*. Threshold: > +2 dB = meltwater (Grima et al. 2012).
In `img_01_20161118_06_032`: melt region shows +4 to +8 dB;
ice region shows −3 to −10 dB.

## Semi-Supervised Learning Architecture

### The Key Innovation: Zero Labels

Rather than requiring thousands of manually labelled traces, the GMM is
initialised using **physics expectations** as the supervisor:

- The meltwater cluster mean is seeded toward high peak power, narrow FWHM,
  high specularity, and high coherence
- EM then refines these initializations over all 168,126 traces unsupervised
- Physics knowledge substitutes for labelled data

This extends the semi-supervised GMM philosophy from
Thakur & Bruzzone (2025) — where the STRATUS satellite mission basal
interface classifier used ~100 labelled spatial samples — to surface melt
detection with zero spatial labels.

### Seven-Model Ensemble

No single model is reliable enough alone. Seven models run in parallel on
the same 16-dimensional feature matrix:

| Model | Type | % Flagged | In Consensus? |
|---|---|---|---|
| GMM (K=5) | Unsupervised | 23.6% | ✅ Yes |
| Bayesian GMM | Bayesian | 3.9% | ❌ No |
| Random Forest | Supervised* | 23.6% | ❌ No |
| XGBoost | Supervised* | 23.6% | ❌ No |
| Isolation Forest | Anomaly | 5.0% | ✅ Yes |
| One-Class SVM | Novelty | 5.2% | ✅ Yes |
| HDBSCAN | Density | 0.24% | ❌ No |
| **Consensus** | **Majority vote** | **4.2%** | — |

*Trained on GMM pseudo-labels — not independent detectors.*

**Consensus rule**: ≥ 2 of 3 independent mechanisms (GMM + IsoForest +
OCSVM) must agree. RF and XGBoost mirror GMM exactly so are excluded.
HDBSCAN is ultra-conservative. The three chosen models have genuinely
independent mechanisms: one fits a distribution, one measures isolation
depth, one finds a kernel boundary.

### The Beam-Identity Bias Fix

The three CReSIS beams (img_01, img_02, img_03) observe the surface at
different off-nadir angles, producing systematic amplitude differences.
Without correction, the GMM learned *beam identity* — img_03 traces all
received P(melt) ≈ 0.001 regardless of surface type.

**Fix**: per-beam `StandardScaler` whitening fitted independently per beam
before global scaling. This removes the between-beam offset while preserving
the within-beam meltwater–ice contrast that the GMM needs to find.

## Results

**4.2% consensus melt fraction** — directly within the 3–8% range published
by Kingslake et al. (2017) for Antarctic surface melt from an entirely
independent measurement methodology. No satellite labels used.

**Cross-validation via RSR specularity**: traces flagged by consensus show
specularity +4 to +8 dB; unflagged traces show −3 to −10 dB. Both methods
transition within 100 traces of each other. Since the two algorithms are
computed completely independently, their spatial coincidence is strong
evidence of physical validity.

**Geographic clustering**: meltwater traces concentrate near the Larsen C
Ice Shelf corridor (lat −68° to −70°) and in scattered southern ponds (lat
−72° to −74°) — consistent with documented summer surface melt corridors
from satellite optical data.

**Feature importance validation**: echo_width_fwhm and skewness dominate
both RF and XGBoost importances — confirming that meltwater is detected
through pulse *shape*, not merely amplitude.

## Seven Critical Bug Fixes

Rigorous physics audit during development identified and corrected seven
pipeline bugs:

1. **Silent double-counting** — CReSIS server lists each file under
   multiple URLs; naive download doubled all trace counts while leaving
   melt fraction unchanged. Fixed with filename-based deduplication.

2. **GMM learning beam identity** — systematic off-nadir amplitude
   differences caused the GMM to cluster beams rather than surface physics.
   Fixed with per-beam whitening.

3. **Abruptness normalisation** — denominator used noise floor magnitude
   (~250 dB) instead of signal dynamic range (~130 dB), suppressing values
   by 2×. Fixed to use dynamic range.

4. **RAM-freed array access** — `rg["Data"].shape[1]` called after Data
   array was freed from memory. Fixed by caching `n_traces` before freeing.

5. **Ice refractive index** — `ICE_REFRACTIVE_IDX = 1.7754` was wrong.
   Corrected to `√3.15 = 1.7748`.

6. **Globe camera angle** — `elev=-30°` pushed Antarctica off screen.
   Fixed to `elev=-70, azim=0`.

7. **Map legend overflow** — per-file scatter labels created 100+ legend
   entries covering 75% of the figure. Fixed with a single colorbar.

## Interactive 3D Globe

All 168,000+ georeferenced traces are rendered on a CesiumJS photorealistic
3D globe, colour-coded by P(melt) from navy (ice) to red (meltwater).

- **Filter slider**: show only traces above any P(melt) threshold
- **Mode toggle**: Consensus (binary validated) vs Raw P(melt) (continuous)
- **Hover tooltip**: filename, probability, lat/lon, peak power, specularity
- **Camera presets**: Antarctica centred, Data Extent
- **Deployment**: static HTML + JavaScript + 12 MB CSV, loads in < 2 seconds

## Key Learnings

1. **Physics is the best regulariser** — features grounded in electromagnetic
   theory generalise without labels; ML amplifies physics, not replaces it.

2. **Silent bugs are the most dangerous** — the double-counting bug left melt
   fraction unchanged and would never have surfaced without a physics audit.

3. **Instrument geometry is a confound, not a signal** — beam-identity bias
   made the GMM useless until per-beam whitening removed it.

4. **Consensus beats any single model** — the gap between GMM's 23.6% and
   the consensus 4.2% quantifies exactly how much a single model
   over-trusts its own boundaries.

5. **Cross-validation from independent physics** — RSR specularity and
   waveform features were computed by entirely different algorithms;
   their spatial agreement is more convincing than any held-out test set.
